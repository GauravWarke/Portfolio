Who survives and who exits

Real business churn from the Australian Bureau of Statistics — how many businesses open, how many close, and how many make it past their first years, across every state. This is attrition at the scale of a whole economy: the same retention question a bank or SaaS firm asks of its customers. No synthetic or modelled numbers.

What this means — in plain English

About 370,500 Australian businesses closed in 2024-25 — a 13.9% churn rate — yet even more opened, so the country still added 66,650 net.

Roughly 1 in 7 businesses exits every year, but 437,150 new ones entered — so the base grew 2.5% to 2.73 million. Survival is the hard part: only 75% make it past year one and under half (48%) reach year three — though employing businesses (61%) and Health Care (82.7%) hold on far better than solo operators (43.3%). Growth is fastest in WA (+4.3%) and Queensland (+3.1%). The call: entries still outpace exits, but the thin survival curve is exactly where retention effort pays — most of all for the 64% of businesses that run with no employees.
