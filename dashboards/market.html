Where the government advertising dollar goes

Every figure here is real, published open data — the Australian Government's own advertising reports plus state auditor and open-data releases. The question: how much do governments spend advertising to the public, on which media, and how is that shifting? No modelled or synthetic numbers.

What this means — in plain English

In 2023-24 the Australian Government spent $250.6 million on campaign advertising — and almost half of the media budget now goes to digital, not television.

This is money the public sector pays to reach citizens: road-safety, health, recruitment and awareness campaigns. Of the $173.8M actually placed in media, 44% went to digital ($75.9M) — now bigger than television ($54.7M). The other $76.6M was campaign development (production, research). Spend swings with the cycle — it was $339M in 2021-22 (COVID), fell to $179M, and rebounded to $250.6M. The call: the media mix has tipped digital-first, so that's where rate negotiation and measurement discipline now protect the most public money.
